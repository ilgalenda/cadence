Material & depth

Glass lives on the floating layer only. Menus, dialogs, toasts, tooltips, the palette, the floating drawer — surfaces that content passes beneath. Everything structural — the field, the rail, cards, wells, tables — is solid. The test for a new surface is one question: does content pass beneath it? Blur is not decoration and it is not free: a handful of elements in the whole platform blur, and the lists that render hundreds of rows deliberately do not. Rebuilt 2026-08-19: the translucent rail and sheet are retired with the frame doctrine.

The floating layer

Menus, dialogs (and the palette, which is a dialog), toasts, tooltips and the floating drawer. Nothing else. The ground below is the product's own content, because that is what these surfaces actually cover — not a photograph, which would flatter the effect and mislead.

Weights

Blur scales with the surface: a large pane reads as thicker glass than a small one. Three steps, chosen by what the surface is, not by taste.

tooltip, chip → thin. menu, toast → regular. dialog, drawer → thick. Nothing structural blurs; the field and the rail are solid white.

Never glass on glass. One floating pane over solid content is the whole budget. Two translucent surfaces stacked lose the legibility both were tuned for — the tint, not the blur, is what keeps text readable, and stacking defeats it.

Which tier

Glass

  • .ds-menu
  • .ds-dialog — and .ds-palette, which is one
  • .ds-toast
  • .ds-tip__body
  • the drawer, in its floating placement only
  • .ds-table thead — the one true scroll-under inside the field

Solid

  • the field and the rail
  • cards, panels, wells, table bodies, rows
  • every list that renders at length — 200+ blurring rows is a defect, not a style
  • the composer
  • anything holding text a person is reading

Rebuilt 2026-08-19. The 2026-08-17 revision made the rail and sheet glass over a gradient ground; both are retired with the frame doctrine. The drawer remains the one component that is both, depending on placement — seated it is solid, floating it is glass.

Translucency is a preference

Honoured structurally in tokens.css, the way reduced motion is, so no component carries its own branch. prefers-reduced-transparency takes the tint to fully opaque and the blur to zero; prefers-contrast: more does the same and promotes the hairline to the defined rule, so every edge is stated rather than implied. Legibility over material, always — translucency is the first thing the system gives up and it is never load-bearing.

The prefix is not optional

The app declares no browserslist and nothing prefixes at build time, so -webkit-backdrop-filter is written by hand beside every backdrop-filter. Without it the material silently disappears on older Safari and the surface falls back to a flat tint — which is legible, but is not what this card describes.
