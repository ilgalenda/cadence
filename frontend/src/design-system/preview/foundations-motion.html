Motion & space

The system's character lives in how it moves. A phase-locked loop settles onto its target; it does not bounce past it and correct. So motion here is critically damped by default — things arrive and stop. Overshoot is permitted in exactly one case: when the user's own gesture carried momentum into it. A flick may overshoot; nothing that merely appeared ever does. Motion is used to reveal structure, confirm an action, or direct attention. Never for spectacle.

Easing

For state that changes on its own clock — hover, focus, colour, a panel opening. Anything a person can interrupt mid-flight wants a spring instead.

Duration

Named for the gesture, not the number — so a change of pace is a change of vocabulary, not a hunt for the right millisecond value.

--dur-tick · 90ms hover, focus — must feel instant

--dur-tap · 140ms press, toggle, checkbox

--dur-shift · 220ms dropdown, tab, accordion, row insert

--dur-reveal · 320ms drawer, panel, dialog

--dur-stage · 460ms route or full-surface change

--dur-beat · 2400ms ambient — the thinking pulse, and nothing else

Springs

For motion a person can interrupt. A transition runs to its target on a fixed clock and cannot be grabbed mid-flight. A spring animates from the value currently on screen, so it can be redirected at any instant, and it can take the velocity of the gesture that released it — which is what removes the seam between dragging something and letting it go.

Stated as damping and response: two numbers to reason about, rather than mass, stiffness and damping. Damping of 1 settles with no overshoot; below 1 it overshoots once and returns. Response is how quickly the value reaches its target, in seconds — not a duration, because a spring has none. Its settle time emerges from the two.

settle · 1.0 / 0.4s the default — anything that appears, repositions or resizes

carry · 0.8 / 0.3s a flick, a throw, a drag release — momentum the person supplied

The rule that keeps this honest: overshoot is earned, never decorative. If the motion did not begin with the user's hand, it uses settle. The two springs live in tokens.css as numbers and are translated in exactly one place — design-system/springs.ts — so a change of doctrine stays a change of tokens.

The beat

The one piece of ambient motion in the system, and the one place the timing metaphor becomes literal. Three ticks at the ambient beat, phase-offset — a metronome counting, not a spinner spinning. It appears only while something is genuinely in progress.

Reduced motion

Honoured structurally. Durations collapse to 1ms rather than 0 — low enough to be imperceptible, non-zero so transitionend handlers still fire and no component's state machine stalls. The ambient beat and the skeleton sweep stop entirely. Springs cannot collapse the same way, since a spring with no response never calls back: springs.ts reads the preference itself and cross-fades instead, keeping the completion callback so nothing waiting on it is stranded.

Radius & elevation

One radius scale: pills for controls, 24px for containers. Elevation is four steps and each means something: seated in the plane, lifted a little, genuinely off it, or above everything over a scrim. A shadow states distance from the plane and is never a border substitute — see Material for which surfaces are allowed to leave the plane at all.
