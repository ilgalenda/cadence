Series colour

For charts, where colour encodes identity — which series — rather than a condition. This is the one place in the system colour does not report state, so it is a separate palette with its own rules. Six slots, assigned in fixed order and never cycled: the order is the colour-blindness safety mechanism, so it never changes.

Derived, not chosen

Brand blue takes slot 1 because the guidelines name it the data-visualisation accent. The rest were stepped in OKLCH and the order searched to maximise the worst adjacent separation. Every slot clears every check in both modes unaided — no labels needed to rescue contrast — so one set serves white sheets and charcoal chrome: lightness band ✓, chroma floor ✓, contrast ≥ 3:1 on both ✓, colour-blind separation ΔE 12.4 worst adjacent, normal-vision floor ΔE 15.0.

Revised 2026-08-17 — the "on both" claim now has a caveat. The rail became charcoal glass over the ground, which lightened it from rgb(26) to rgb(39). Re-measured against that: slots 2, 3 and 5 land at 2.88:1, 2.92:1 and 2.96:1 — a hair under the 3:1 floor. On the sheet all six still clear it, tightest slot 4 at 3.06:1.

So: charts render on the white field, never on charcoal. That was already the practice and the reason these figures were quoted against the sheet in the first place — it is now a rule rather than a habit. If a chart is ever needed on chrome, the palette must be re-derived against the glass rail; do not simply place it there and hope.

Why it runs cool

Green, amber, red and gold are absent by design. They are reserved as locked drift fault canon — and a status colour must never impersonate a series. Every series hue therefore sits at least 34° of hue away from all four.

The cap — and it is a hard one

Six slots Bars, lines and stacked segments. Only neighbouring marks touch, so adjacency safety is what matters and all six are separable.
Three slots Scatter, bubble, node-link graph, map — any form where any two marks can end up side by side. Only the first three separate, and even those need a second channel such as a direct label. This is a property of colour vision, not of the palette: no re-ordering or re-stepping raises it.
Never Generate a seventh hue, or cycle back to slot 1. A seventh series folds into --series-other, becomes a small multiple, or takes a different encoding entirely.
Never Colour a series by its value. Bar length already shows magnitude; spending the identity channel to repeat it wastes both.
Never Let a filter repaint the survivors. Colour follows the entity, never its rank — a series keeps its slot when its neighbours disappear.
